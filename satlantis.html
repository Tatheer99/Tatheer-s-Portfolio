01 / Context

A social layer for discovering places and communities.

Satlantis brings social content, place discovery, merchant profiles, and community events into one experience. Instead of treating these as separate destinations, the product connects them so people can move naturally between people, places, businesses, and events.

Design question

How can a content-rich discovery product make people, places, businesses, and events feel connected while keeping each experience easy to navigate?

02 / Contribution

My role & scope.

Product/UI/UX Designer across the responsive product experience.

  • Product flows and information architecture
  • Desktop and mobile interface design
  • Social, discovery, merchant, and event experiences
  • Responsive adaptation and content hierarchy
  • Reusable cards and interaction patterns

03 / Social experience

Making discovery social.

The social experience combines a content feed with profiles, reactions, comments, engagement metrics, suggested creators, collections, followers, and following. Business and event content can also appear within the feed, connecting social activity to local discovery.

  1. 01

    Profiles built around activity

    User profiles bring together bio, posts, followers, following, collections, and events so people can understand both the person and their activity.

  2. 02

    Social proof in context

    Ratings, recommendations, followers, mentions, comments, and attendee information help people make decisions with more context.

  3. 03

    Connected content

    Posts, merchants, places, and events can lead users into related parts of the product rather than ending at a single piece of content.

04 / Place discovery

Discovery that starts with where you are.

Place discovery supports both nearby exploration and destination-based search. Users can browse merchants, open a map entry point, search by place, and refine results through detailed filters.

Design decision 01

Keep the default experience simple

Nearby and destination controls provide a clear starting point, while more detailed filters stay available when users need them.

Design decision 02

Make detailed filtering useful

Filters cover categories, ratings, Bitcoin acceptance, dietary needs, atmosphere, and other attributes without forcing every option into the initial discovery view.

The trade-off: More filtering options improve precision, but too many visible controls can make discovery feel like a form.

05 / Merchant & events

Giving local information enough depth.

Merchant and event experiences needed to support detailed information without losing the social context around them.

Merchant profiles

Business descriptions, galleries, ratings, categories, opening hours, contact details, websites, addresses, amenities, community recommendations, bookmarks, and similar merchant suggestions are organized into a clear profile.

Events as community experiences

Event pages combine date, time, location, host, attendees, registration, Interested actions, venue details, announcements, discussions, comments, and related merchant information.

Connections between experiences

Merchants, events, places, and social content are designed to connect with one another so users can continue exploring instead of reaching a dead end.

06 / Responsive design

Adapting dense discovery for smaller screens.

The product was designed across desktop and mobile, with the mobile experience restructuring dense information into a more vertical hierarchy rather than simply shrinking the desktop layouts.

  • 4 desktop screens
  • 8 mobile screens
  • Responsive content hierarchy
  • Mobile-friendly navigation and filters
  • Prominent actions for follow, register, Interested, search, filter, and map access

07 / Reflection

Designing discovery as a connected system.

The main challenge was organizing a broad set of social and local discovery features without making the product feel fragmented. The design connects people, businesses, places, and events while keeping the primary actions visible and the information hierarchy predictable.

What this project strengthened

Satlantis pushed the product across several content types and interaction models, from social feeds and profiles to location-aware discovery, detailed merchant information, filtering, event registration, and community discussion.
